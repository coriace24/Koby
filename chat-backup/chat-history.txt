----- CLAUDE  (2026-08-09 20:59 UTC) -----
Good question — the sign and the color answer two *different* questions on purpose.

## The − / + sign: which direction is the gap?

The Difference column is always **Documents minus Manual entry**:

- **− (minus)** = the documents show *less* than what you typed into the calculator
- **+ (plus)** = the documents show *more* than you assumed

It's pure arithmetic, no judgment. In your screenshot, effective gross income is −$254,155: the property's actual documents show about a quarter-million dollars *less* income per year than your manual assumptions.

## The red / green color: is that gap good or bad *for you as the buyer*?

Color is favorability, and that's why it doesn't simply follow the sign:

- For income, NOI, cap rate, DSCR, cash flow, cash-on-cash → **higher is better**, so a minus is red (reality underperforms your assumptions) and a plus would be green.
- For **operating expenses, the logic flips** → lower is better. That's your green row: documents show **−$145,664** of expenses — the property actually costs far *less* to run than you assumed, which is *good news*, hence green despite the minus sign.

So: **sign = direction of the gap, color = whether that direction helps or hurts the deal.**

## Reading your screenshot "at the end of analysis"

The table is telling a clear story: your manual assumptions describe a far richer property than the documents do — $428K assumed income vs $174K documented, giving an implausible 20.7% cap rate and 57.8% cash-on-cash on the manual side, versus a believable 9.9% / 16.1% from the documents.
